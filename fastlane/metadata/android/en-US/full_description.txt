Ripple is an internet-free messenger for nearby Android and iOS phones. It has no servers and the Android app does not request the INTERNET permission.

Messages travel phone-to-phone over Bluetooth Low Energy. Multi-hop flooding lets a phone relay packets for peers that cannot reach each other directly, while store-and-forward keeps packets for peers that reconnect later.

Features:
• A public channel for everyone nearby
• End-to-end encrypted direct messages
• Signed packets and delivery acknowledgements
• Background mesh operation
• Opt-in SOS beacons, with location attached only when you choose
• Out-of-band identity verification with QR identity codes and 12-digit safety codes
• Passphrase-encrypted identity backup and restore
• Battery profiles, a simulated neighbourhood, guided field tests, and exportable diagnostics

Ripple's QR codes are render-only. The app has no camera permission: scan with any QR app and paste the decoded identity or backup code into Ripple.

Ripple is open source. The current v1 engine has conformance and simulation coverage but still needs more real-device field testing; review the documented security trade-offs before relying on it for high-risk use.
